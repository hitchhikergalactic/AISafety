This document describes the causal logic behind the work of iaS (Inteligencia Artificial Segura), that is, how and why our activities produce the change we seek; it complements the [Vision](/en/vision), which explains what iaS is and where it is heading, and it builds on the activities detailed in [What we do](/en/que-hacemos).

---

## 1. The problem

Spanish is spoken by more than 650 million people, about 520 million of them as a native language, spread across the five continents ([Instituto Cervantes, 2025](https://cervantes.org/es/sobre-nosotros/sala-prensa/notas-prensa/espanol-crece-30-millones-hablantes-ano-5-anterior)); yet the decisions about how the most advanced AI is developed, deployed and controlled are made mostly in English. We identify three gaps, ordered by their weight:

1. **A talent gap.** Reducing the serious risks of AI requires many more people who understand them and work on them from policy, research, law, journalism and industry. The reference training, including that of **BlueDot Impact**, is delivered in English, and the Spanish-speaking world had no sustained entry point in Spanish; iaS has opened it, and more than 1,000 people have taken part in its events since February 2026. This is the main gap, and the one our activities address most directly.
2. **A governance gap.** Spain applies the European Union's AI Act, which imposes specific obligations on general-purpose models with systemic risk, and several Latin American countries are legislating on AI. Those who make these decisions need counterparts in their language who understand the risks of advanced AI; iaS has already taken part in the European Commission's public consultation on the classification of high-risk systems under the AI Act.
3. **An evidence gap.** High-resource languages such as Spanish resist translation attacks much better than minority languages ([Yong et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02446); [Deng et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.06474v3)). However, safety evaluations are designed in English, studies such as [M-ALERT](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15035) show differences by risk category, and the regional varieties of Spanish have barely been studied. That question is under-measured, and measuring it is part of iaS's work.

## 2. Our theory

iaS holds that lasting change in AI safety in the Spanish-speaking world comes from three elements that we consider insufficient in Spanish, namely, evidence of our own, people who understand the risks and know how to act, and a stable organization that institutions and media can talk to; the most effective way to build them is a common infrastructure that operates entirely in Spanish, that informs the public, that accompanies people from their first exposure to a concrete contribution and that produces citable evidence and content along the way.

In short: **if we build in Spanish the infrastructure that is missing (training, evidence, network and institutional presence) and make it easy to enter, we will produce the people and the knowledge that make AI safer for everyone.**

### Assumptions behind the theory and how we test them

- **A1. A significant share of Spanish-speaking talent does not enter the field because of the language barrier or the lack of a nearby network.** Test: the entry form asks whether the person would have attended an equivalent activity in English.
- **A2. First exposure leads to deeper engagement.** Test: we measure the share of attendees who move on to Discord, the AGI Strategy Course, a BlueDot course, a fellowship or a position in the field.
- **A3. Spanish-speaking institutions turn to local organizations to understand the risks of advanced AI.** Test: we record the consultations, invitations and institutional citations we receive.

## 3. The logic model

### Inputs

| Input | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Partnership with BlueDot Impact** | Curriculum of the Course on Strategy for Artificial General Intelligence and joint monthly seminars |
| **Own operating model** | Open online sessions (first and third Wednesday of each month), Luma calendar, Discord and a CRM that records registrations, attendance and ratings |
| **Brand** | iaS, with the tagline "Inteligencia Artificial Segura. En español". |
| **Team** | Multidisciplinary team that brings together management, design, writing, management of the network on Discord, translation and legal advice |
| **Evidence base** | Multilingual studies on safety and bias and a papers radar |
| **International network** | The hub in [Mexico](https://www.aismx.org/), [AI Safety Colombia](https://aisafetycolombia.org/) and [BAISH](https://www.baish.com.ar/es) in Buenos Aires, with which we work closely; ENAIS, AISafety.com and local AI safety groups in other countries |
| **Digital infrastructure** | Own website, Discord, the "Semana iaS" newsletter, YouTube channel, LinkedIn and Instagram |
| **Status** | Nonprofit organization, in the process of formal registration as an NGO |

### Activities

Organized according to the blocks of [What we do](/en/que-hacemos):

- **Train**: the [AI safety Seminar](/en/seminario-bluedot-spain), with **BlueDot Impact**, and the [AGI Strategy Course](/en/curso-estrategia-agi), cohort-based and with no technical requirements.
- **Connect**: a calendar of open sessions and social meetups on Luma, and ongoing discussion on the iaS Discord.
- **Research**: [Paper digest](/en/biblioteca-papers) and translation of materials.
- **Communicate**: a weekly newsletter, videos and social media posts that bring the evidence and news of the field into Spanish.
- **Chapters**: [iaS Valencia](/en/delegaciones/valencia) as the first chapter, and a procedure for [starting new chapters](/en/delegaciones/crear-una-delegacion).
- **Advocacy**: taking part in public consultations and in institutional forums.

### Expected outcomes

1. **An evidence base in Spanish.** iaS measures how AI systems behave in this language and its varieties, and takes that evidence to regulators, developers and media, who cite it as a source.
2. **A pathway for talent.** People who go from their first session to training, contributing and working in AI safety (policy, research, industry or civil society), without language being a barrier.
3. **Greater literacy and public debate in Spanish.** AI safety enters the conversation of universities, companies and media across the Spanish-speaking world, and those who take part in it act with better judgment.
4. **A network of chapters.** The model proven in the online sessions is replicated in other cities and countries of the Spanish-speaking world, starting with Valencia, with a shared brand, materials and procedures.
5. **Institutional partnerships.** Universities, administrations, civil society organizations and companies turn to iaS for training, collaboration and dialogue on AI safety.

### Impact

**That the transition to advanced AI systems does not cause catastrophic or irreversible harm, and that the Spanish-speaking world contributes people, institutions and decisions to prevent it.**

### How we measure it

Today, the iaS CRM records the registrations, attendance and rating of each event, which tells us how many people come back and how they rate the sessions. But reach is not change, and we have started measuring change: every event includes an entry form and an exit form, which record the weekly hours each person spends on AI safety and whether they would have attended an equivalent activity in English (assumption A1). We also follow up after six months with those who attend two or more events, and in the CRM we record each step a person takes to the next level of the pathway: Discord, the AGI Strategy Course, BlueDot courses, fellowships or positions in the field.

## 4. Evidence and validation

**The problem is documented.** Multilingual studies such as M-ALERT (see section 1) show differences in behavior across languages, which vary by risk category.

**There is demand.** Since February 2026, more than 1,000 people have taken part in iaS's open events, with an average rating of 4.56 out of 5, with 95% of the ratings at 4 or 5.

**People come back.** 416 people have attended two or more events.

**What we are measuring.** Career transitions show up over the medium term; the entry and exit forms and the six-month follow-up test assumptions A1 to A3 and measure the change attributable to iaS, and the chapter model will be evaluated with the results from Valencia.

## 5. Scaling logic

The model produces a **cycle** at the center of the theory:

- More sessions and more chapters, more people entering the field in Spanish.
- More people, more data of our own on what the public wants and what retains it, and more citable content and evidence.
- More evidence and more data, more credibility with institutions and funders.
- More credibility, more resources for materials, programs and chapters.
- More resources, more chapters interested in joining, and the cycle repeats.

This cycle works because AI safety in Spanish combines two needs that no local group covers on its own: presence in each city, which makes in-person meetings and local roots possible, and an infrastructure shared across the whole Spanish-speaking world, which makes possible the common language, the evidence, the brand and dialogue with institutions and funders; Spanish is, in this sense, the infrastructure that unites the five continents.

For a new chapter, iaS provides the brand, the materials, the Discord, the registration form and the management of funding applications, so that those who lead it do not have to pay for events out of their own pocket or start from scratch; in this first phase, the brand, the funding and the attendee data are managed centrally from iaS, and the model will be reviewed according to the results in Valencia.

Beyond its own chapters, iaS works closely with the hub in Mexico, with AI Safety Colombia and with BAISH in Buenos Aires, and its aim is to help expand the nodes across Spanish-speaking countries, so that those that do not yet have their own can open one.

The role of iaS is to catalyze and sustain this cycle: to maintain the quality of the central infrastructure, to support chapters through their difficulties and to keep the mission at the center as the organization grows.
